Hearing Aids

Like for Real, Really Cool

Hearing Aids — Like for Real, Really Cool is a graphic design and research project examining how stigma—especially internalised stigma—shapes the experiences of hard of hearing (HoH) individuals. While hearing aids are often framed through medical or corrective narratives, users frequently navigate a complex social landscape where visibility, identity, and self-perception collide.

The project draws from qualitative research including user interviews, social media observation, and field visits to hearing aid centres. These methods surfaced personal accounts of embarrassment, adaptation, pride, and the subtle negotiations that accompany everyday device use. The aim is not to romanticise hearing aids, but to understand how confidence, resistance, and self-presentation emerge around them.

Through curated stories, quotes, and visual insights, the project challenges prevailing assumptions and repositions hearing aids as empowering artefacts rather than markers of deficiency. By amplifying users’ voices and aesthetics, the work advocates for a cultural shift toward broader acceptance—one where hearing aids are recognised as legitimate expressions of identity, style, and technological agency.
